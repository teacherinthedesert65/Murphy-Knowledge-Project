A Year in a Mock Mars Habitat: NASA’s CHAPEA Study

What can a year on simulated Mars teach us before people travel to the real planet?

ADDED NOVEMBER 9, 2026 • APPLICATION STATUS CHECKED NOVEMBER 9

Applications are currently closed. NASA’s participation page says recruitment for the second one-year mission has closed and directs readers to check back for future opportunities. A recent headline about volunteers should not be taken as confirmation that a new application window is open.

Life inside an Earth-based Mars habitat

CHAPEA stands for Crew Health and Performance Exploration Analog. At NASA’s Johnson Space Center in Houston, four volunteers live in an isolated, 3D-printed habitat measuring about 1,700 square feet. Private quarters and shared spaces support living, research, exercise, maintenance and crop-growing activities.

The study examines health and performance under simulated exploration conditions, including isolation, limited resources, equipment problems and heavy workloads. Crew activities include simulated spacewalks, science, food preparation and habitat upkeep. NASA uses the findings to inform food systems, resource requirements and risk tradeoffs for long missions.

Who has been eligible?

NASA’s published criteria specify U.S. citizens or permanent residents aged 30–55, a STEM master’s degree or an accepted alternative, and relevant professional experience. Listed alternatives include certain doctoral study, medical degrees and test-pilot training; some bachelor’s-degree applicants with additional qualifications may also be considered.

Selection includes medical and psychological screening and suitability for prolonged isolation. Compensation is available for selected participants. The official page contains further dietary, medication and screening requirements; future calls may change these criteria.

Where the program stands

The first mission ran from June 25, 2023, to July 6, 2024. Mission 2 began October 19, 2025; NASA’s October 27, 2026 advisory scheduled its crew’s exit for October 31 after 378 days. Those dates describe the existing mission program, rather than a newly verified recruitment round.

MKP perspective: test the human system

A habitat proposal needs more than walls and equipment. It also needs a workable daily routine: enough time to maintain systems, prepare meals, exercise, solve problems and recover. CHAPEA offers a useful comparison for MKP’s settlement discussions: what should be tested in a small Earth-based habitat before committing people to a distant outpost?

An analog can explore selected challenges while leaving others unresolved. Its value comes from stating precisely which questions the experiment can answer.
