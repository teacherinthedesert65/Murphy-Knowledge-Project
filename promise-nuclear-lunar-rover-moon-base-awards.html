PROMISE: NASA’s Nuclear-Powered Lunar Scout and New Moon Base Awards

A planned lunar rover reuses Mars hardware to explore the south pole, alongside new delivery, communications and surface-power projects.

ADDED NOVEMBER 8, 2026 • NASA ANNOUNCEMENT NOVEMBER 7

A scout for the lunar south pole

PROMISE stands for Polar Rover for Observation, Mapping, and In-Situ Exploration. NASA plans a late-2028 launch and an 18-month primary mission. The rover will investigate lunar terrain and resources to inform future Moon Base operations.

JPL is adapting Curiosity’s duplicate test vehicle, supplemented with spare and test hardware from the Curiosity and Perseverance programs. A flight-spare multi-mission radioisotope thermoelectric generator (MMRTG) will power the vehicle during lunar darkness, including exploration of permanently shadowed regions.

What “nuclear-powered” means

The power system converts heat from the natural radioactive decay of plutonium-238 into electricity. It also provides useful heat in cold environments. This is radioisotope power, rather than a controlled fission chain reaction.

Prospecting before extraction

PROMISE’s drill will sample material down to about one meter. Spectrometers, cameras, temperature and radiation sensors will characterize the environment; advanced computing and autonomous navigation will also be demonstrated. Prospecting helps establish what resources exist and where. It does not mean the rover is an industrial water-ice mining operation.

What else NASA announced

  • Delivery: Blue Origin received a $94 million base award for PROMISE delivery, with a performance-based option worth another $140 million.
  • Commercial landers: Eight companies can compete under CLPS 2, a ten-year framework with a combined $10 billion ceiling. The existing CLPS ceiling rises from $2.6 billion to $4.2 billion.
  • Transportation: Six task orders, together approximately $1.3 billion, support launch and orbital-transfer demonstrations, including navigation testing and robotic scouting.
  • Communications: SpaceX was selected for a cislunar communications deployment demonstration using Starship in 2028.
  • Surface infrastructure: miniCAST component awards cover energy storage, solar generation and wireless power transfer. NASA anticipates a separate integrated-power-system solicitation in early 2027.

Read the funding numbers carefully: A contract ceiling is the maximum available under a framework, not money already committed to every eligible company. Options depend on their terms, and launch dates remain targets.

MKP perspective: building a working system

The wider significance is how these pieces fit together. A resource map becomes useful when vehicles can reach the site, power equipment and return data. For MKP’s Earth–Moon–Mars discussions, this offers a concrete question: which combination of scouting, transport, energy and communications should be demonstrated first to reduce the greatest uncertainty?
