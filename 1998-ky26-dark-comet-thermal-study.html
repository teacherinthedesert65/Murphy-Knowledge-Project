1998 KY26: Thermal Modeling Challenges a Dark-Comet Explanation

A new Icarus study tests whether buried ice can survive in the small near-Earth asteroid targeted by Hayabusa2’s extended mission.

ADDED NOVEMBER 10, 2026

Testing a proposed source of acceleration

Asteroid 1998 KY26 is a target of Japan’s Hayabusa2 extended mission, with a planned rendezvous in 2031. A study by Korbin Hansen and Masatoshi Hirabayashi in Icarus examines whether its thermal environment could preserve the volatile material needed for an outgassing explanation.

The “dark comet” concept concerns small bodies with nongravitational acceleration but without the conspicuous dusty appearance of familiar comets. Escaping gas is a proposed source of the extra force.

What the model challenges

The study reports that KY26’s orbit-averaged temperatures are unfavorable for long-term survival of water ice. This weakens a sustained buried-water-ice explanation for its motion. The result is a thermal-model constraint, rather than an on-site inventory of the asteroid’s interior.

A narrower conclusion: Challenging one proposed mechanism does not identify the correct mechanism, or rule out the entire dark-comet category. The planned spacecraft encounter remains in the future.

MKP reflection: explanations need independent tests

An orbit can reveal that an additional force is needed without revealing its cause. Thermal modeling asks a different question: could the proposed fuel survive? A useful explanation needs to satisfy both constraints.

What measurements would be most informative during a close encounter—surface temperature, composition, rotation, escaping gas, or a better shape model? Each could resolve a different part of the puzzle.

Primary research

Hansen and Hirabayashi, Thermally unfavorable conditions of volatile existence in near-Earth asteroid 1998 KY26, the Hayabusa extended mission’s target, Icarus, available online in 2026.
