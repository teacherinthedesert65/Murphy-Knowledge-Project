Voyager 1 Reaches One Light-Day as Science Data Falls Silent

NASA's most distant spacecraft is still sending a radio signal, but its scientific and engineering telemetry is missing.

MKP RESEARCH EXPLAINER • NOVEMBER 24, 2026

What happened?

On November 14, NASA detected a radio carrier from Voyager 1 through its Canberra Deep Space Network station, without usable telemetry. Engineers suspect the computer that prepares instrument and engineering readings for transmission. The specific fault remains unconfirmed. CNN reported on November 24 that NASA said the outage was continuing.

A historic milestone

Voyager 1 crossed the one-light-day threshold on November 18, 2026, at approximately 25.9 billion kilometers (16.1 billion miles) from Earth. NASA confirmed the achievement the following day using radio reception and trajectory models.

Why troubleshooting is difficult

A command takes approximately 24 hours to reach the spacecraft. A response takes another 24 hours to return, making each test a two-day undertaking at minimum. The received carrier indicates that the antenna pointing and radio transmission systems remain functional, but does not prove that every subsystem is healthy.

Lessons from an earlier failure

A similar problem in 2023 was traced to the flight data subsystem. Engineers restored science transmission in 2024. That experience informs the current investigation, although it does not establish that the same hardware has failed again.

MKP perspective

Launched in 1977, Voyager 1 entered interstellar space in 2012. Its longevity illustrates the value of dependable hardware, redundant systems and remote repair capability. Future interstellar probes could benefit from autonomous diagnostics and fault recovery because communication delays make continuous human supervision impractical. The present outage is not proof that the mission has ended, nor is recovery guaranteed.
