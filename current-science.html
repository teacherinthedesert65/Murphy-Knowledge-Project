One CRISPR Infusion Cut LDL Cholesterol by 52.5% at One Year

A first-in-human Phase 1 trial of CTX310, an experimental CRISPR-Cas9 therapy targeting the liver gene ANGPTL3, followed 15 adults with difficult-to-treat lipid disorders. At the highest dose, mean LDL cholesterol was 52.5% below baseline and triglycerides 47.8% lower after 12 months. The result is striking, but this remains a very small early-stage trial: it was designed primarily to study safety and dosing, not to prove prevention of heart attacks or strokes.

How Current Science works

This page is intentionally selective. New findings will be added when they are interesting, well sourced, and useful to the larger Murphy Knowledge Project. Early results will be identified as early results, animal studies will not be presented as human treatments, and intriguing chemistry will not be called evidence of life unless the evidence actually supports that conclusion.
