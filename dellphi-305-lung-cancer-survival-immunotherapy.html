MEDICINE • IMMUNOTHERAPY • PHASE 3 HUMAN EVIDENCE • OCT 25, 2026

New Immunotherapy Combination Extends Survival in Aggressive Lung Cancer

Evidence status: Randomized Phase 3 trial demonstrating improved overall survival in a defined cancer population. This is disease-specific life extension, not generalized reversal of aging.

The study

DeLLphi-305 randomized 563 people with extensive-stage small-cell lung cancer whose disease had not progressed after initial durvalumab, platinum chemotherapy and etoposide. Participants received maintenance durvalumab alone or durvalumab combined with tarlatamab, a DLL3-targeted T-cell engager.

Results

OutcomeTarlatamab + durvalumabDurvalumab alone
Median overall survival from maintenance randomization21.1 months12.7 months
Estimated alive at 12 months79%53%
Median progression-free survival7.4 months1.9 months

The overall-survival hazard ratio was 0.42 (95% CI 0.32–0.54; p<0.001), corresponding to a 58% relative reduction in the instantaneous hazard of death during follow-up—not a guarantee that any individual patient's risk falls by exactly 58%. The median survival difference was 8.4 months. Results came from a planned interim analysis, with approximately 16 months median overall-survival follow-up.

How does it work?

Tarlatamab is a bispecific antibody that brings CD3-bearing T cells into contact with tumor cells expressing DLL3. Durvalumab blocks PD-L1, reducing an immune-inhibitory signal. Together they aim to enhance anti-tumor immune activity.

Safety and limits

Grade 3 or 4 treatment-related adverse events were reported in 20.9% of patients receiving the combination and 10.8% receiving durvalumab alone. Cytokine-release syndrome is an important recognized risk of tarlatamab and requires appropriate clinical monitoring. The combination as first-line maintenance treatment was investigational at publication; approval for other uses of a component does not imply approval of this regimen. Longer follow-up will clarify durability and late risks.

What it means for healthspan

Unlike biological-age clock changes, the primary outcome here is actual survival. That makes this a significant therapeutic advance for eligible people with an aggressive malignancy. It does not show that healthy people age more slowly, that the immune system is broadly rejuvenated, or that a general longevity treatment has been discovered.
