How Saharan Dust Helps Feed the Amazon

Wind links a desert and a rainforest through a transatlantic delivery of mineral nutrients.

ADDED NOVEMBER 1, 2026 • RESEARCH: 2015 • SATELLITE RECORD: 2007–2013

Where the headline number comes from

A study led by Hongbin Yu, published in Geophysical Research Letters in 2015, used CALIPSO observations from 2007–2013 to estimate dust deposition over the Amazon Basin. Its best annual average was 27.7 teragrams: 27.7 million metric tonnes, not US short tons. This is a historical multiyear estimate, rather than a measurement for 2026.

How a satellite follows dust

NASA explains that CALIPSO’s lidar sends light pulses into the atmosphere and measures the returning signal. Optical properties help distinguish dust from other particles and reveal its vertical distribution. Winds carry the dust across the Atlantic; some settles or is removed by rain along the way.

Why phosphorus matters

The researchers estimated roughly 22,000 metric tonnes of phosphorus delivered annually with the dust, comparable to estimated losses through runoff and flooding. That external input can help replenish nutrients over long timescales. Internal recycling through fallen plant material is much larger, and other atmospheric sources also contribute phosphorus.

Read the estimate with its uncertainty

The paper gives a broad dust-deposition range of about 8–48 million tonnes per year when accounting for its estimated ±70% uncertainty. Year-to-year variability is a separate issue. The authors also state that the dust supply needed to maintain forest productivity remains unknown. The headline number is a useful scale estimate, not a fixed yearly delivery or a complete nutrient budget.

Why it belongs in MKP

MKP interpretation: An ecosystem’s boundaries on a map do not necessarily match the boundaries of its supporting processes. Studying the Amazon involves looking beyond its trees and rivers to atmospheric transport and distant sources of material.

This is also an example of why a compelling number needs context: the units, measurement period, method and uncertainty all shape what it means.

Classroom investigation

Draw a system map with a dust source, atmospheric transport, ocean deposition, forest deposition, plant uptake and nutrient recycling. Mark which quantities you would need to measure to build a complete phosphorus budget.

  • How is replacing a nutrient loss different from supplying all plant demand?
  • Why might a seven-year average differ from any individual year?
  • What extra measurements could narrow the uncertainty?
