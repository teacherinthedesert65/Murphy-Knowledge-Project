Rebuilding the Lighthouse of Alexandria in a Digital World

Recovered monumental stones help researchers reconstruct a lost ancient wonder.

MKP ARCHAEOLOGY • NOVEMBER 27, 2026

Recovering an ancient wonder

Although Futura reported the story on November 27, 2026, the recovery of 22 stone blocks occurred in June 2025, as confirmed by CNRS. The monumental doorway fragments include jambs, lintels and a threshold; some weigh 70–80 metric tonnes. Other finds include foundation slabs and fragments of a previously unidentified Egyptian-style gateway constructed using Greek techniques.

The original lighthouse

Built in the early third century BCE under the Ptolemies, the Pharos guided ships into Alexandria's harbor. Earthquakes damaged the structure over centuries. Its site is now occupied by the Fort of Qaitbay, with extensive architectural remains offshore.

A digital archaeological puzzle

Researchers led by Isabelle Hairy combine overlapping photographs into three-dimensional models through photogrammetry. The PHAROS project integrates physical measurements, historical descriptions and engineering analysis to test possible arrangements of the fragments.

A new reconstruction

The Dassault Systèmes Foundation reports a reconstructed height of 103 meters, an entrance reaching 13 meters and a proposed interior with 67 rooms across nine levels. These dimensions describe the research team's reconstruction, not an intact surviving building. Digital simulations can test whether proposed structures are consistent with load paths and archaeological evidence.

MKP perspective

Digital twins make ancient engineering hypotheses testable without physically rebuilding fragile remains. Researchers should distinguish measured fragments from inferred geometry and publish alternative reconstructions when evidence is incomplete.

Discussion question

How could engineers use stone dimensions, material strength and earthquake simulations to test competing reconstructions of the lighthouse?
