Icy Moons: How Radiation Builds New Chemical Bonds

Laboratory clusters reveal a pathway to peroxide chemistry, without demonstrating life.

ADDED OCTOBER 28, 2026

What the experiment tested

At Berkeley Lab’s Advanced Light Source, researchers exposed clusters of carbon dioxide and water to vacuum ultraviolet light. They measured the resulting ions and compared them with calculated reaction pathways.

Why water matters

Water-rich clusters favored formation of an oxygen–oxygen bond. The surrounding molecules helped stabilize the reacting system: a few nearby water molecules can change which reactions become possible.

What this means for icy moons

The result offers a molecular explanation relevant to peroxide chemistry on irradiated icy surfaces. It was a laboratory cluster experiment, not a direct measurement of reactions on a moon or a complete simulation of an ocean world.

The evidence boundary

Chemical energy can matter for habitability, but this experiment does not demonstrate organisms, an inhabited ocean, or the origin of life.
