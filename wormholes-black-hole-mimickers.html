Could Wormholes Mimic Black Holes?

A fascinating theoretical possibility, and what it would take to test it.

ADDED OCTOBER 20, 2026 • THEORETICAL PHYSICS

Evidence status: theoretical models

This story concerns hypothetical alternatives to black holes. The cited research does not report the discovery of an astronomical wormhole. A model that resembles a black hole observationally is not evidence that the object actually exists.

Could a wormhole look like a black hole?

In their 2007 paper, Thibault Damour and Sergey Solodukhin investigated a hypothetical wormhole without an event horizon. They showed that it could closely reproduce several behaviors associated with black holes, including accretion and the ringing of disturbances. Their purpose was to examine how observations could distinguish objects with different underlying geometries. Original paper [1]

What does “mimic” mean?

Imagine two objects producing nearly identical signals in a particular measurement. That measurement alone may not tell you which object produced the signal. Researchers construct alternatives to learn which additional measurements would discriminate between them. Mathematical resemblance does not establish a natural formation process, long-term stability, or safe passage for spacecraft.

What could distinguish them?

A 2024 study analyzed late-time wave behavior in Damour–Solodukhin wormhole models, including tails and echoes after the main ringing signal. The authors examined how these theoretical features could help distinguish the models from black holes. This is a prediction to investigate, rather than a reported detection of a wormhole. Waveform study [2]

A headline worth checking

The Futura article dated October 20, 2026 prompted this entry. Its headline describes a “new theory,” but the explicit wormhole-mimic example it discusses is the 2007 Damour–Solodukhin paper. The publication date of a news story is not necessarily the date of its underlying scientific idea. Read the triggering article [3]

Why this matters

This question connects gravity, spacetime geometry and the limits of measurement. It is a strong MKP topic because it invites readers to distinguish a possibility, a testable prediction and an observation. It does not justify saying that black holes have been revealed as cosmic travel tunnels.

Questions for discussion

  • Which observations could rule out a particular wormhole model?
  • Could different models produce a similar echo?
  • What would explain how the proposed object forms and remains stable?
