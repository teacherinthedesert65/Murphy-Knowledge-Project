Underwater Eruption Adds 125 Acres to Iwo Jima

Satellite observations document new land forming along a historic Japanese island.

MKP RESEARCH EXPLAINER • NOVEMBER 25, 2026

A growing island

NASA Earth Observatory reported on November 24 that an offshore eruption at Ioto (Iwo Jima) created approximately 50 hectares (125 acres) of land, increasing the island's area by an estimated 1.7 percent. The deposits extend the historic southeastern beach.

Timeline

  • October 27: seismic unrest and eruption observations.
  • October 28: NISAR radar identifies a disturbance despite cloud cover.
  • November 11: Landsat 9 records the new coastline, heat and floating pumice.
  • November 13: Japan Coast Guard observes explosions and ash rising approximately 300 meters.
  • November 16: confirmation that the new deposits connect to the existing island.

Will the new land survive?

Loose volcanic fragments, known as tephra, are vulnerable to wave erosion. Lava may consolidate the deposits, improving their resistance. A similar addition in 2023 disappeared within weeks; the durability of the latest land remains unknown.

MKP perspective

This event provides a natural experiment in coastal geomorphology. Combining radar measurements with optical imagery can help researchers separate erosion from subsidence and continued deposition. Repeated observations could test whether the composition of fresh volcanic deposits predicts their longevity.

Discussion question
