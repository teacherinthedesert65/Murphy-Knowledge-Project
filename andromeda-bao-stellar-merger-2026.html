When Two Stars Become One: Andromeda’s Bao Stellar Merger

A rare luminous red nova reveals how a giant star engulfed its companion.

MKP RESEARCH EXPLAINER • NOVEMBER 26, 2026

A rare encounter in Andromeda

About 2.5 million light-years away, an expanding supergiant engulfed a smaller orbiting star. The event, AT 2025abao, was reported in November 2026. The larger star was estimated to have seven solar masses, while the companion had less mass than our Sun.

How the merger unfolded

Over roughly seven years, the system changed as the companion spiraled inward. Drag within the giant's gaseous envelope transferred orbital energy and angular momentum to surrounding material. On October 17, 2025, observers detected an eruption classified as a luminous red nova, distinct from a conventional supernova.

Evidence from multiple telescopes

Archival NEOWISE data, ground-based observations and SPHEREx measurements helped establish the earlier evolution. Webb observed the aftermath 47 days after peak brightness, using infrared measurements to investigate surrounding dust. Combining these observations allowed researchers to test simulations of the merger.

Why this matters

Binary stars can exchange mass, eject a shared envelope or merge. These outcomes affect predictions of stellar populations and compact-object formation. Bao offers a rare observational test of common-envelope evolution. Whether the merged object will appear rejuvenated over the long term remains a question for future monitoring.

MKP perspective

The strength of this discovery is the combination of observations across wavelengths and years, rather than a single dramatic image. Most of the physical sequence is reconstructed from measurements and models; artist illustrations are not direct photographs of the stars colliding.

Discussion question

How could repeated infrared spectroscopy distinguish an expanding dust shell from continuing mass loss by the merged star?
