The Treasure of Villena: Bronze Age Objects Made With Iron From Space

Chemical analyses support a meteoritic origin for two iron objects in Spain’s famous hoard, linking ancient craftsmanship with meteorite material.

ADDED NOVEMBER 9, 2026 • ANALYSIS PUBLISHED ONLINE JANUARY 19, 2024

Two unusual pieces in a celebrated hoard

Discovered in 1963 near Villena in Alicante, Spain, the Treasure of Villena includes two iron objects that long complicated interpretations of its age: an open bracelet or ring and a hollow hemisphere covered with decorated gold sheet.

Salvador Rovira-Llorens, Martina Renzi and Ignacio Montero-Ruiz examined their chemistry using portable X-ray fluorescence and laser-ablation inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry. The results favor meteoritic iron for both objects, supporting a Late Bronze Age context before local production of iron from terrestrial ores.

How chemistry tests the space connection

The researchers compared nickel, iron and cobalt relationships with meteoritic materials and considered trace elements including gallium, germanium and ruthenium. Similar trace-element patterns suggest a potentially similar source, but they do not establish a particular named meteorite.

Corrosion matters: the samples came from weathered surface oxides, whose composition can differ from the original metal. The authors discuss the need for additional analyses to understand those effects.

What the finding concerns: Evidence for meteoritic iron applies to these two iron objects. It does not mean the hoard’s gold was identified as meteorite metal. The study belongs to the journal’s 2023 volume and appeared online in January 2024.

MKP perspective: a material is not a complete history

This is a striking meeting of archaeology and planetary science. Yet identifying a material’s likely origin answers only part of the historical question. Who acquired it? How did it move between communities? What did its makers believe about it? Chemistry can constrain explanations without recovering every step in an object’s journey.

A useful discussion question is what evidence would connect these pieces to a specific meteorite—or show that their makers recognized the material as something that fell from the sky. Neither conclusion follows automatically from a meteoritic composition.
