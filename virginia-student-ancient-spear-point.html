A Search for Shark Teeth Reveals an Ancient Virginia Spear Point

Mary Baldwin student Brooklyn Rolph found a six-inch stone point on an ecology field trip, with an estimated age of 3,000–4,500 years.

ADDED NOVEMBER 15, 2026

An unexpected discovery

On September 21, 2026, Mary Baldwin University biology student Brooklyn Rolph was searching for shark teeth during an ecology field trip on Virginia’s Eastern Shore. At an inlet’s edge, she spotted a stone point about six inches long. She alerted her professors.

What archaeologists identified

According to the university’s October 22 account, state archaeologists identified the artifact as a Lehigh/Koens-Crispin spear point and estimated its age at 3,000–4,500 years. The full range matters: “up to 4,500 years old” does not mean its age is precisely known.

The account does not provide a laboratory dating result or a detailed archaeological analysis. This article therefore reports the identification and age range as the university describes them, without assigning a particular maker or reconstructing how the point reached the inlet.

What happened to the artifact?

The university reports that the island is state-owned preserve land and the class visited under a state-issued permit. Conservation staff collected the point and delivered it to the Virginia Department of Historic Resources for further study.

MKP reflection: A discovery becomes a research question

Finding an object is the beginning of its scientific story. Its material, manufacturing marks, surroundings, and comparison with other artifacts can support different kinds of conclusions. An attractive headline compresses that process; a careful account keeps the uncertainties visible.

This also illustrates the value of field learning. Students can practice observation in one discipline and encounter questions that cross into another. The educational opportunity is not just recognizing something unusual, but learning whom to consult and what evidence is still needed.

Discussion question: What can a loose artifact reveal on its own, and what questions would require information about its original archaeological context?
