Alexandr Wang Says Superintelligence Is Arriving: What Does the Riemann Proof Really Show?

Meta's AI chief sees a turning point in an AI-assisted solution to a 167-year-old mathematical problem. The evidence is remarkable—but the predictions require separate scrutiny.

MKP RESEARCH & POLICY ANALYSIS • NOVEMBER 23, 2026

What Wang said

In a November 22 essay titled Superintelligence is Arriving, Meta Chief AI Officer Alexandr Wang argued that humanity is approaching artificial superintelligence and that decisions made over the coming year may have far-reaching consequences. He pointed to the claimed AI-assisted proof of the Riemann Hypothesis as evidence that AI is rapidly gaining extraordinary intellectual capabilities. Wang urged greater attention to safety, alignment, security and maintaining human control rather than accepting an automatic handover of societal decisions to machines.

The 167-year-old mathematics problem

Proposed by Bernhard Riemann in 1859, the Riemann Hypothesis concerns the nontrivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function and their relationship to the distribution of prime numbers. It is one of the Clay Mathematics Institute's Millennium Prize Problems. A successful proof would be a landmark in mathematics, irrespective of whether it was developed by a person, AI or both.

What happened in November 2026?

On November 20, mathematician Levent Alpöge, working with Anthropic's Claude, announced a claimed proof and released a very large formalization in the Lean proof assistant. A separate, human-written proof claim by Alessandro Maria Masullo also surfaced around the same time. The AI-assisted work received substantial technical scrutiny: the Lean Focused Research Organization and Mathlib Initiative publicly expressed confidence in the formal verification, according to Scientific American. Nonetheless, broader mathematical understanding, examination and official prize recognition are distinct from automated checking. As reported by Nature, the rival proof claims require rigorous evaluation.

Evidence check: The AI-assisted proof has been reported as independently machine-checked and strongly supported by proof-verification specialists. That is substantially stronger than an unreviewed AI answer. It does not establish that every mathematician has independently understood the argument, that all formal recognition processes have concluded, or that artificial superintelligence has arrived.

Why is this an AI milestone?

The work suggests AI can contribute at the frontier of highly abstract research, especially when combined with human mathematical direction and formal verification tools. It also demonstrates why independently checkable outputs matter: mathematical claims can be subjected to exact formal checks in ways many other AI outputs cannot.

Does a mathematical breakthrough prove superintelligence?

No. Solving an extremely difficult problem is evidence of impressive specialized capability, but the definition of artificial superintelligence involves broad performance beyond humans across many domains. It does not follow automatically from one result, even an extraordinary one. The pace of future progress, autonomous research capability and reliability outside formal mathematics remain open empirical questions.

Wang's concern: control and recursive improvement

Wang argues that increasingly capable AI could automate AI research itself, producing faster improvements than human institutions can oversee. He urges the industry to emphasize safety, alignment and security. The concern deserves examination, but recursive self-improvement at the scale Wang envisions is a forecast, not an observed inevitability. Different researchers disagree about likely timelines and the effectiveness of proposed safeguards.

MKP analysis

The strongest conclusion is that AI-assisted mathematics has reached an important new threshold, with an unusually transparent route for checking the result. A second, separate conclusion is that governance questions become more urgent as capabilities improve. Neither conclusion requires declaring that humanity has already lost control or that all scientific research can now be automated.

The next evidence to watch includes expert mathematical explanations of the formal proof, confirmation of its implications, additional independently replicated AI research achievements and demonstrated safety controls for increasingly autonomous systems.
