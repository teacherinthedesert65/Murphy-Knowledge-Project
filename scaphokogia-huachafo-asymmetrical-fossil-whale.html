Scaphokogia huachafo: Peru’s Remarkably Asymmetrical Fossil Whale

A newly described Late Miocene whale reveals an extraordinary skull and clues to a specialized life feeding near the seafloor.

ADDED NOVEMBER 9, 2026 • STUDY PUBLISHED NOVEMBER 9, 2026

An ancient relative with an extraordinary face

Scaphokogia huachafo is a newly described extinct member of the pygmy sperm whale family, Kogiidae, from Peru’s Pisco Formation. It lived about 6.3–5.6 million years ago and was approximately 2.7 meters long. Lead author Aldo Benites-Palomino examined the fossil during his doctoral research at the University of Zurich; he is now at Friedrich-Alexander-Universität Erlangen-Nürnberg.

What made its skull so unusual?

The study’s three-dimensional shape comparisons identify exceptional facial asymmetry in Scaphokogia. Its paired bony nasal openings lie far to the left, while surrounding facial structures also differ markedly between sides. The asymmetry extends beyond the nasal region familiar in toothed whales.

Keep the claim precise: The researchers describe the most extreme facial asymmetry documented among cetaceans, and potentially among mammals. “The most crooked mammal ever” is a stronger claim than the paper establishes.

A head built for bottom feeding?

A heavy, tubular, downward-pointing snout and reconstructed skull–neck articulation suggest that the animal could direct its head toward the seabed. The authors interpret these features as adaptations for specialized feeding in shallow coastal environments. Using echolocation to search sediment for prey is a proposed behavioral explanation, rather than something directly preserved in the fossil.

New description, older discovery

The skull was recovered in the mid-1990s, and Benites-Palomino encountered it in a Lima museum in 2015. The university reports that the team found no fractures or pathological changes explaining its unusual shape, supporting a biological origin rather than simple distortion during fossilization. The species name huachafo draws on a Peruvian Spanish term for something extravagant or over-the-top.

MKP reflection

This fossil invites a useful distinction: an unusual shape is an observation; the lifestyle inferred from it is an explanation to test. How could comparisons with living animals strengthen that explanation? Which other fossils would help distinguish a feeding adaptation from another function?
