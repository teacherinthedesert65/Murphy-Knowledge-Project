Shenzhou-20: A Cracked Window and the Lessons of Orbital Debris

A damaged capsule returned without astronauts after 270 days in orbit.

MKP RESEARCH EXPLAINER • NOVEMBER 24, 2026

What happened

Today's Futura Sciences retrospective recounts an emergency that began in November 2025, not a new incident in November 2026. Cracks in the Shenzhou-20 capsule's window raised concerns about safe atmospheric reentry. Chinese authorities identified an impact by orbital debris as the probable cause, rather than a conclusively identified particle.

Protecting the crew

The original crew landed aboard Shenzhou-21 on November 14, 2025. An uncrewed replacement, Shenzhou-22, launched on November 25 to restore a reliable evacuation option at Tiangong. Astronauts examined the damaged window during a December 9 spacewalk and installed equipment intended to improve thermal protection and sealing.

Return to Earth

The damaged Shenzhou-20 landed uncrewed on January 19, 2026, at the Dongfeng landing site. Officials reported that the exterior was generally intact and the returned contents were in good condition. Its extended mission lasted 270 days.

Why orbital debris is dangerous

Even tiny particles can carry substantial kinetic energy at orbital encounter speeds. Tracking and collision avoidance help with larger objects, but smaller particles are harder to track. Shielding, inspection and redundant systems remain important. A successful uncrewed landing does not establish that the earlier decision to prohibit a crewed return was unnecessary.

MKP perspective

Future orbital stations and lunar missions should treat emergency transportation, compatible docking systems, inspection capability and spare equipment as fundamental design requirements. This episode illustrates how planning for failures can protect human lives without abandoning scientific operations.
