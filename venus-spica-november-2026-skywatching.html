Venus at Peak Brilliance: A November 2026 Skywatching Guide

See brilliant Venus near Spica before sunrise.

MKP SKYWATCHING • NOVEMBER 27, 2026

See the morning star

On November 28–29, look east to southeast about 60–90 minutes before local sunrise. Venus shines near Spica, the brightest star in Virgo. From typical northern midlatitudes, Spica appears to the right or upper right of Venus. Exact orientation and altitude vary with location. Choose an unobstructed horizon and clear skies. No telescope is needed.

When is peak brightness?

Space.com places maximum brightness on November 27, while EarthSky places it on November 29. Published brightness estimates also vary, from magnitude −4.4 to −4.9. The practical conclusion is the same: Venus is exceptionally bright throughout the weekend, and differences are small to the unaided eye.

Why a crescent can shine so brightly

Venus reflects sunlight from its clouds. Its apparent brightness depends on distance, illuminated fraction and scattering by its atmosphere. After inferior conjunction in October, its growing illuminated crescent and still-large apparent disk combine to produce exceptional brilliance. Spica is a distant binary star; the apparent proximity is a line-of-sight coincidence.

Observing safely

Use unaided eyes or a steady smartphone before sunrise. Never point binoculars or a telescope toward the Sun. Mercury may be visible lower in the twilight, depending on location and conditions.

MKP perspective and discussion
