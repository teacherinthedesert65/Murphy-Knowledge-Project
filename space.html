CONFIRMED DISCOVERY • OCT 20, 2026

Euclid’s Unexpected Find: A New Comet

ESA announced on October 19 that Euclid had discovered its first previously unknown comet, C/2026 S1 (Euclid). Images from September 16–24 revealed a moving streak with a fuzzy coma of dust and gas; follow-up observations led to Minor Planet Center confirmation on October 8.

Why it matters: A telescope built to investigate dark matter and dark energy through distant galaxies can also reveal nearby Solar System objects. Detection software and follow-up astronomy turned a survey’s moving foreground object into a discovery.

Keep the claim precise: This is Euclid’s first discovery of a new comet, not its first observation of any comet. The detection process could support future Comet Interceptor searches, but ESA says this particular comet is not a suitable target. Its orbital estimate will improve as observations accumulate.
