Rare Yellow Burbot Reveals a Conservation Mystery in Lake Ontario

An unusually colored native fish offers a rare opportunity to investigate the health of a declining population.

MKP RESEARCH EXPLAINER • NOVEMBER 25, 2026

An unexpected discovery

In October 2026, federal fisheries biologists surveying Oswego Harbor caught a bright yellow-orange burbot (Lota lota). This cold-water native fish normally has mottled brown or olive coloration. The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service announced the discovery on November 10.

The genetics of color

The agency identified xanthochromism, an unusual pigmentation condition in which reduced dark coloration allows yellow and orange pigments to dominate. The agency's description of the catch as exceptionally rare should not be interpreted as a measured frequency of one in a million.

What the veterinary examination revealed

The fish was severely underweight and was sent to Cornell University's College of Veterinary Medicine. According to Smithsonian's November 24 report on a federal wildlife office update, examination revealed carcinoma, skin lesions and a mild kidney parasite infection. It had also stopped feeding. There is no established causal connection between its unusual pigmentation and cancer.

Why conservation scientists care

Burbot populations in Lake Ontario are unusually low. This was the first recorded burbot capture in an official fisheries survey of Oswego Harbor, although anglers had previously reported catches. The species generally favors colder, deeper water. Monitoring programs use multiple sampling techniques, including environmental DNA, to improve detection of uncommon species.

MKP perspective

One specimen cannot establish the causes of a population decline. Comparing disease prevalence, age, environmental exposures and habitat conditions across representative samples would help distinguish individual illness from broader ecological problems. The discovery illustrates how routine monitoring can create unexpected research opportunities.

Discussion question

How could researchers distinguish genetic susceptibility to disease from environmental causes in a declining fish population?
