Taters the Cat: A Laser Video From 19 Million Miles Away

NASA’s 2023 deep-space video test shows how laser links can carry more data while remaining bound by the speed of light.

ADDED NOVEMBER 16, 2026 • HISTORICAL MILESTONE: DECEMBER 11, 2023

A cat video with an engineering purpose

On December 11, 2023, NASA’s Deep Space Optical Communications (DSOC) experiment aboard Psyche sent a 15-second ultra-high-definition video across nearly 19 million miles. Its star, an orange tabby named Taters, chased a laser pointer. The clip had been loaded before launch; the cat stayed on Earth.

A near-infrared laser carried the encoded data to the Hale Telescope at Caltech’s Palomar Observatory in California. The test reached 267 megabits per second, the system’s maximum rate. This was a technology demonstration, rather than a promise of that speed on every future link.

Two meanings of “fast”

NASA’s educational breakdown separates transmission time from travel time: sending the 153.6-megabit file took about 0.58 seconds, while the signal needed another 101 seconds to cross space. The video’s 15-second playback length is a third, different quantity.

Lasers and radio waves both travel at light speed. Optical links can increase the amount of information carried each second; they cannot remove the delay imposed by distance. A narrow beam also demands precise aiming, including accounting for Earth’s motion while the signal travels.

Why MKP is following this

Space exploration depends on getting observations home. Better communication capacity could make richer imagery and larger scientific datasets more practical. Taters makes an abstract engineering test memorable, but the useful question is what the link demonstrated under its particular test conditions.
