Sibling Supernova Remnants? The IC 443 Binary-System Candidate

Two remnants, one proposed history

Researchers propose that IC 443, the Jellyfish Nebula, and nearby G189.6+3.3 originated from two stars once belonging to the same binary system. This is an interpretation of surviving remnants, not a live observation of two stars exploding simultaneously.

What connects them?

The study combines 16 years of Fermi gamma-ray observations with eROSITA X-ray and other wavelength data. Evidence of interactions with shared interstellar clouds places the remnants in the same physical environment and supports a common distance.

How a binary could leave two explosions

Two massive stars can evolve and explode at different times. The authors compare inferred separation and timing constraints with binary-evolution models, finding a scenario consistent with the observations. A shared origin remains a candidate explanation rather than an established certainty.

What the headline overstates

The finding offers a rare possible link between two remnants. It does not mean binary stars or supernovas were previously unknown, nor that stellar physics has been overturned. The journal paper was published July 21, 2026; October coverage revisits earlier research.
