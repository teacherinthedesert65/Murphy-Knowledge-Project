Comet 41P: Evidence of a Spin Reversal

Escaping gas can change a small world’s rotation—and perhaps its future.

ADDED OCTOBER 28, 2026 • NASA RELEASE MARCH 26, 2026 • OBSERVATIONS FROM 2017

The evidence

David Jewitt analyzed archival Hubble observations of 41P/Tuttle–Giacobini–Kresák from December 2017. Brightness variations support a rotation period of 0.60 ± 0.01 days—about 14.4 hours. Earlier Swift measurements indicated 46–60 hours. The study interprets this change as a likely reversal driven by outgassing torque.

The nucleus’s effective radius is estimated at 500 ± 100 meters. Its small size makes outgassing particularly important to its rotational evolution.

How gas jets can turn a comet

Solar heating causes ice to become gas and escape. Uneven jets exert torque: a turning effect. A persistent torque opposing the original spin can slow rotation through zero and build rotation in the other direction.

Spin is different from orbit

“Backward” refers to rotation about the comet’s own axis. It does not mean the comet reversed its path around the Sun.

Observation, interpretation and prediction

Evidence levelWhat the claim means
MeasurementsRotation-period estimates changed dramatically during 2017.
InterpretationReversal offers a natural explanation of the combined measurements; the transition was not continuously filmed.
PredictionFurther spin-up could destabilize the nucleus, causing fragmentation.

Will it self-destruct?

NASA reports Jewitt’s expectation of rapid future destruction, but this is not a witnessed breakup or a firm countdown. The paper notes that unusually strong activity could make estimated lifetimes too short; alternatively, the present nucleus may be a remnant of a larger body. Future activity matters.

Why it belongs in MKP

MKP perspective: This story connects a familiar physics idea—torque—with a changing natural object. It also shows why old observations remain valuable: a later question can reveal meaning in an existing dataset.
