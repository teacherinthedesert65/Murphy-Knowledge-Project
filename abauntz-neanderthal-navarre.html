Abauntz: A Neanderthal Hand Opens a Window into the Pyrenees

Six fossil fragments from northern Spain connect anatomy, molecular evidence and deep time.

MKP EXPLAINER • OCTOBER 20, 2026

What was announced?

On October 19, 2026, researchers presented the first identified Neanderthal remains from Navarre, Spain. The fossils come from Abauntz Cave in Arraitz-Orkin, in the Ultzama Valley. “First” refers to identified fossils in this region, not the first Neanderthal discovery in Spain or the world. The announcement date should not be confused with the excavation date; the bones were recovered during earlier campaigns.

What did researchers find?

The reported assemblage consists of six bone remains associated with an adult male Neanderthal: three metacarpals and a phalanx from the right hand, plus two fragments identified through protein analysis. Direct radiocarbon dating places the remains at approximately 44,000–49,000 years old, commonly summarized as about 46,000 years.

Why this belongs in MKP

Small fossils can provide several independent kinds of evidence. Anatomy helps identify skeletal elements; preserved proteins can help classify fragments; radiocarbon measurements constrain their age; and ancient DNA can reveal biological relationships. The research team reports good DNA preservation, with further analysis continuing.

A geographic gap in the fossil record

The fossils add direct evidence of Neanderthals in Navarre. Abauntz lies near routes linking the two sides of the Pyrenees, making the site useful for investigating prehistoric population movement. This geographic setting motivates questions about connections between populations; the discovery alone does not establish a specific migration route or encounter.

What remains unknown?

Ongoing genetic work may clarify how this individual relates to other Neanderthal populations. The announcement does not by itself establish why Neanderthals disappeared, whether this individual encountered Homo sapiens, or what caused his death. Those questions require additional evidence.

MKP takeaway

The value is the combination: fossils with archaeological context, direct age measurements and preserved molecular evidence. A hand from Abauntz can help researchers investigate the final millennia of Neanderthal history in western Europe.
