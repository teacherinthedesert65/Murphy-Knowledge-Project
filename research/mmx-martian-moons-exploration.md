# Japan's MMX Mission: Bringing a Piece of Phobos to Earth

*Murphy Knowledge Project | Space Exploration | Updated October 18, 2026*

Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency (JAXA) is developing the Martian Moons eXploration (MMX) mission to investigate Phobos and Deimos, the two moons of Mars. Its central goal is to collect material from Phobos and return it to Earth for laboratory study—the first planned sample return from a Martian moon.

## Why Phobos?

Scientists are still debating how Mars acquired its moons. They may be captured asteroids, or they may have formed from debris produced by a giant impact involving Mars. Chemical and mineralogical analysis of returned Phobos samples could help distinguish between these possibilities and illuminate the early history of the Martian system.

## Mission plan

MMX is designed to enter the Martian system, observe both moons, study Phobos at close range, collect surface samples, and bring those samples back to Earth. A small rover, developed with European partners, is planned to explore Phobos's surface. The mission includes international contributions from NASA, ESA, CNES and DLR.

## What the samples might reveal

Phobos's surface may contain particles thrown off Mars by impacts. Such grains could potentially preserve information about Mars's ancient surface, although detecting and identifying them would require careful analysis. This is not the same as collecting samples directly from Mars, and it would not by itself establish the existence of past life.

## Questions to follow

- Do Phobos and Deimos share a common origin?
- What are the chemical and isotopic signatures of Phobos's material?
- Can scientists identify any grains originating on Mars?
- How do low-gravity conditions affect surface sampling and rover mobility?
