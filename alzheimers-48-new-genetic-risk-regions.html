Alzheimer’s: 48 New Genetic Risk Regions

A larger map of disease associations provides research leads—and calls for careful interpretation.

ADDED OCTOBER 28, 2026 • PUBLISHED IN NATURE, OCTOBER 28, 2026

Numerical verification status (October 30, 2026): The counts below are transcribed from the study summary used to prepare this article. What the study found

Uffelmann and colleagues analyzed 2,815,127 participants across 30 cohorts. They identified 118 loci in a multi-ancestry analysis plus nine in ancestry-specific analyses: 127 regions, including 48 new ones.

Proxy cases reported parental Alzheimer’s; they were not all personally diagnosed. The effective sample size was approximately 416,493, with 91% European ancestry, so diversity remains a limitation.

Biological clues

Risk associations were enriched among genes upregulated in microglia and downregulated in neurons in cases versus controls. This supports investigating immune-cell and neuronal processes together; it does not establish one universal causal chain.

Drug-target claims: what is supported?

The authors identified 17 genes interacting with drugs approved for other conditions. Earlier research offered animal-model promise for RRM2B, SYK and KEAP1; some approaches targeting CACNA1S and AXL had unsuccessful results. The discussion also cites promising mouse evidence involving AXL, so results depend on the particular intervention and model. These are research leads, not demonstrated Alzheimer’s therapies.

Reading “risk region” correctly

A locus is a genomic region. An association does not automatically identify a causal variant, a single responsible gene or a treatment. Genetic risk changes probability; it is not an individual diagnosis or a guarantee of disease.

From a signal to a useful therapy

MKP interpretation: A useful way to read this research is as a sequence of questions: Is the association reproducible? Which biological process explains it? Can changing that process help? Does an intervention improve outcomes in people? Success at one stage does not answer the next.

Corrections to the shared summary

The published journal is Nature. “48 new regions” should not become “48 proven causes.” Nor should five previously investigated targets all be presented as successful or equally promising. A drug’s approval for another condition does not establish its benefit for Alzheimer’s.

Why this matters for healthy longevity

MKP perspective: Preserving cognition is central to healthspan. A more detailed research map can improve the questions scientists ask, while meaningful progress toward treatment still needs functional experiments and clinical evidence.
