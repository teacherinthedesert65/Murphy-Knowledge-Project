The Future of Human Longevity

More healthy years—not merely more years. Can advances in biology help people retain strength, cognition, independence and purpose later in life? Geroscience studies how the mechanisms of aging influence many diseases at once. Research is advancing, but dramatic human rejuvenation remains unproven.

1. Biology of aging

Cellular senescence, mitochondrial dysfunction, chronic inflammation, DNA damage, altered nutrient sensing and the twelve hallmarks of aging.

2. Cellular rejuvenation

Senolytics and partial epigenetic reprogramming may modify age-associated cellular changes. Benefits for human lifespan and long-term safety are not established.

NIH research context ↗

3. Regenerative medicine

Stem-cell treatments, tissue engineering and organ repair range from established therapies for particular conditions to experimental interventions.

4. Cardiovascular longevity

Blood-pressure control, LDL reduction, exercise, smoking avoidance and evidence-based cardiac care protect healthspan. Supplements claiming plaque reversal need rigorous trials.

5. Brain and cognitive health

Investigating dementia prevention, physical activity, sleep, hearing, vascular risk and the emerging biology of neurodegeneration.

6. Society and longer working lives

Healthy longevity raises questions about retirement, lifelong learning, access to care, disability, inequality and meaningful work at older ages.

Research tracker: what is actually known?

Established human health benefits: cardiovascular risk reduction, appropriate exercise, vaccinations and condition-specific medical treatment.

Early human or mixed evidence: senolytics, some metabolic interventions, NAD-related supplements and biological-age biomarkers. A changed biomarker does not prove longer life.

Primarily preclinical or highly experimental: partial epigenetic reprogramming for broad rejuvenation and proposed combinations to reverse systemic aging.

Not established: that nattokinase, rapamycin, metformin or other drugs extend lifespan in otherwise healthy humans. Do not change prescribed medicines based on preliminary longevity claims.
