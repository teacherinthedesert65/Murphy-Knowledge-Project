AI and Quantum Gravity: Chandrasekaran’s Null-Cone Proposal

An AI-assisted preprint proposes a mathematical framework for vacuum gravity.

MKP RESEARCH EXPLAINER • NOVEMBER 27, 2026

Can AI help reconcile gravity and quantum mechanics?

Venkatesa Chandrasekaran's November 25 preprint, An algebraic quantization of gravity from the null cone (arXiv:2611.34924), proposes a quantum description of four-dimensional vacuum general relativity. News reports describe months of collaboration with GPT-6 Astra and approximately 400 million tokens of computation. The sole listed author is also affiliated with OpenAI, which supplied computing resources.

Why null cones?

A null cone describes the possible paths of light extending from a spacetime event. Intersecting light cones bound a causal diamond. Rather than presupposing a fixed spacetime geometry, the proposal constructs quantum observables from gravitational data on these boundaries. In broad terms, it replaces classical relationships between variables with quantum operator relations.

What the paper claims

The preprint reports a finite result for a graviton-pair process whose perturbative expansion diverges. It also claims a finite-dimensional state space for a size-bounded causal diamond, recovery of black-hole entropy and its known one-loop logarithmic correction, and a semiclassical Einstein equation in the low-energy regime. These are reported mathematical results, not independently validated findings or experimental measurements.

What remains unresolved

The proposal treats vacuum gravity, not the full set of matter fields and interactions. It has not undergone peer review. Reproducing known physical limits is a useful consistency test but does not establish that a theory is unique or empirically correct. Physicists including Jonathan Oppenheim have raised objections to the work; others see it as an interesting preliminary investigation. Neither enthusiasm nor criticism alone settles the mathematical questions.

MKP perspective: human accountability

AI may accelerate symbolic exploration, conjecture generation and checking of intermediate steps. Token consumption alone is not a measure of mathematical reliability. Independent specialists should examine assumptions, reproduce derivations and seek counterexamples. Disclosing the model, compute support and division of labor is important, especially when the author is affiliated with the model provider.

Discussion question

What independent mathematical checks and possible observational predictions would distinguish a promising quantum gravity framework from an established theory?
