Dolly Parton’s Deep-Sea Legacy: A New Antarctic Scale Worm

A newly described scale worm connects deep-ocean biodiversity with a musical legacy.

MKP RESEARCH EXPLAINER • NOVEMBER 27, 2026

A new species from Antarctic waters

Lenka Neal and Helena Wiklund formally described Bathyadmetella dollypartonae in Taxonomy on November 17, 2026. The first specimen was collected in the Amundsen Sea in 2008 at 1,020 metres. The largest examined specimen was 14 mm long.

A tribute to Dolly Parton

The researchers chose the name to commemorate Parton's artistic and charitable achievements after her death. The worm's elongated pale appendages also evoked her distinctive hairstyle. Expedition records describe the animal as purple before alcohol preservation altered its appearance; no photographs of living specimens are known to the researchers.

What the DNA reveals

This is the second named species in its genus, following B. commando in 1967. DNA from a damaged specimen collected near the East Scotia Ridge supports identification of the same species roughly 4,000 kilometres away. The COI sequence divergence between sites was 0.003, or 0.3%. This suggests a potentially broad distribution, not a demonstrated continuous range or known population size. The molecular study supports distinction from the related genus Admetella, while deeper evolutionary relationships remain uncertain.

MKP perspective

The interval between collection and description illustrates the scientific value of museum collections and the need for sustained taxonomic research. Establishing baseline species distributions can help assess future environmental change in Antarctic ecosystems. The discovery does not itself establish how this worm responds to warming.

Discussion question

How could standardized seabed sampling and genetic analyses establish whether the species is widespread or restricted to particular habitats?
