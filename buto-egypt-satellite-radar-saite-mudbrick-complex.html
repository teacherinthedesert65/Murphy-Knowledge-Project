Satellite Radar Helps Reveal a 2,600-Year-Old Egyptian Complex

Modern remote sensing helps uncover the engineering ingenuity of Egypt's Saite period.

MKP RESEARCH EXPLAINER • NOVEMBER 23, 2026

Discovery at Buto

A March 2026 study in Acta Geophysica describes buried architecture at Kom C, a mound at Tell el-Fara'in (ancient Buto) in the northwestern Nile Delta. The researchers associate the structure with the 26th Dynasty, in the seventh and sixth centuries BCE.

How technology guided excavation

Sentinel-1 radar imagery helped identify an area worth investigating. Researchers then measured electrical resistance in the ground and reconstructed subsurface patterns using three-dimensional modeling. Radar did not directly photograph the buried building. Excavation of a 10-meter-square area verified the existence of mudbrick walls.

Ancient engineering

The survey suggests a building footprint of roughly 20 by 25 meters, with architectural remains several meters beneath the surface. An underlying sand deposit is interpreted as intentionally prepared groundwork. Sand might have helped distribute loads or improve drainage on clay-rich terrain, although the precise engineering functions remain uncertain.

What was the building used for?

Objects recovered during excavation include religious amulets and ritual items. A temple, associated service building, priestly residence or funerary facility are possible interpretations. More excavation is needed to establish its original function.

MKP perspective

This research illustrates how complementary measurements can reduce uncertainty before costly excavations. It also highlights the practical challenges ancient builders faced in the Nile Delta. Future surveys could reveal additional occupation layers and clarify how the settlement evolved.
