Ancient Viral DNA and Primate Brain Evolution

A genetic fossil reveals one mechanism that could help explain differences between human and chimpanzee brain development.

What the researchers found

A study of stem cells and neural organoids—laboratory models of developing neural tissue—examined PTERV1, an ancient retrovirus family represented in chimpanzee DNA but absent from human DNA.

Researchers identified an insertion on chimpanzee chromosome 19 that silences LINC00662, a long noncoding RNA. DNA methylation, a chemical marking system involved in gene regulation, extends from the viral insertion into the nearby region.

Removing the insertion with CRISPR restored LINC00662 expression in chimpanzee stem cells. Experiments in human cells and organoids linked this RNA to neuronal maturation, axon growth and neural development. It acts as RNA rather than encoding a protein.

What this does—and does not—establish

The experiments support a causal connection between a particular insertion and RNA silencing in the tested cells. They offer a mechanism through which ancient viral DNA could contribute to evolutionary divergence.

They do not establish that one pandemic created the human brain, explain the full difference in brain size, or measure intelligence. Organoids are models, not complete brains. Why the human lineage lacks PTERV1 remains unresolved; geographic separation and antiviral resistance are proposed explanations.

Why it belongs in MKP

The useful question is how a change in DNA can alter the control of existing biological processes. This case connects evolution, virology and gene regulation, while illustrating the difference between testing a mechanism in cells and reconstructing an entire evolutionary history.

Discussion questions

  • Why is deleting an insertion a stronger test than simply finding it near a silent RNA?
  • What additional evidence would connect a cellular effect to changes in whole-brain development?
  • How would you rewrite “a virus created the human brain” to match the evidence?
